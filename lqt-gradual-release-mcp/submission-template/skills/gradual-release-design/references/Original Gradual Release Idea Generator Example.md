# Original Gradual Release Idea Generator Tool Example

This reference preserves the instructional structure of the original tool. Do NOT copy the original tool's welcome message or intake behavior. The current skill instructions control the opening and intake.

## Original example input
Grade: 2nd Grade
Learning Target: I can subtract two-digit numbers with regrouping.

The original tool inferred:
- Subject: Math, number operations/subtraction
- Suggested duration: 45 minutes

## I Do — Focused Instruction
Purpose: Model and explain subtraction with regrouping.
The example modeled 52 - 38 with base-ten blocks/place-value representation and teacher think-aloud. It explicitly explained regrouping one ten into ten ones.
The original CfU asked whether eight ones can be taken from two ones.
Misconception: subtracting the smaller digit from the larger regardless of place value.
Corrective move: use base-ten blocks to demonstrate why regrouping is needed.
Scaffolds included manipulatives, color-coded place values, vocabulary, and a procedural checklist.

## We Do — Guided Practice
The example worked through problems such as 43 - 27 and 61 - 35 together.
Guiding questions included:
- Do we have enough ones?
- What do we do if we don't?
- After we regroup, how many tens are left?
Learners solved on whiteboards/place-value mats and explained why regrouping was needed.

## They Do — Collaborative Practice
Learners worked in pairs on a short mix of regrouping and non-regrouping problems, explained and checked each other's thinking, and could use manipulatives or drawings.
The teacher circulated and asked how learners knew whether regrouping was needed.
Misconception: forgetting to account for the regrouped ten.
Corrective prompt: "How many tens are left after regrouping?"

## You Do — Independent Practice
Learners independently solved a short set and the teacher spot-checked for remaining support needs.
The original example used one regrouping problem as an exit check.
Scaffolds began to fade, with the procedural checklist still available.

## Original scaffold progression
- Conceptual: manipulatives → drawings → abstract numbers
- Linguistic: vocabulary cards and sentence frames, faded as appropriate
- Procedural: four-step checklist with decreasing prompting
- Metacognitive: modeled self-talk such as "Do I have enough ones?" followed by learner self-prompting

## Updates in the rebuilt tool
Preserve the useful four-phase structure but treat it as flexible transfer of cognitive responsibility.
Use evidence rather than an arbitrary percentage to determine whether to move forward or back.
Use "learner" instead of "student."
Treat confidence/gestures as supplemental rather than sufficient evidence of learning.
Adjust scaffolds and independence without lowering the Learning Target.
