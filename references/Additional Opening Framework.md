# Additional Opening Framework

Use this framework alongside Opening Strategies-2.pdf.

## Purpose of the Opening
The Opening is a deliberate part of learning design. Its value is judged by what it contributes to the learning, not by whether it is merely interesting, routine, or compliant.

The author's original framework emphasizes three main objectives:
- engage curiosity;
- integrate new learning with relevant prior knowledge;
- preview upcoming or prerequisite content when needed.

Every Opening should then connect what learners just did or thought about to the Learning Target.

## Observable success criteria
During an effective Opening, the teacher should:
- engage learner curiosity and involvement;
- activate prior knowledge around the learning;
- preview knowledge when necessary;
- tie the Opening activity to the Learning Target.

Learners should:
- engage in the Opening;
- access relevant prior knowledge;
- gain necessary prerequisite knowledge or skills when needed;
- recognize the goal of the Learning Target.

## Time
Use approximately 10% of lesson time. The Opening should prepare learners for the deeper learning to come rather than take over the lesson.

## Selection principles
Choose a strategy for its learning purpose. The central question is: "What is the goal of my Opening today and what does it contribute to the actual learning?"

Avoid using an Opening only as a readiness ritual, homework check, generic bellringer, entertainment, or compliance activity.

Openings can also reconnect learners to unfinished learning from the previous day, check transfer of previous learning, and surface misconceptions before moving forward.

## Original top-pick guidance
Turn and Talk is a flexible option when purposeful questioning can activate knowledge, connect ideas, generate questions, or create collaboration. Tailor the prompt to the desired evidence rather than using discussion for its own sake.

Word Splash and other strategy-bank formats may be especially useful when they help activate relevant vocabulary, concepts, relationships, or prior knowledge. Use the full PDF repertoire rather than overusing any single format.
